Life insurance holds a special place in wealth transfer in France: unlike most other assets, it generally falls outside the civil rules governing estates while still being subject to its own dedicated tax regime. Understanding how life insurance works after a death makes it possible to anticipate the amount actually transferred to beneficiaries and to avoid unpleasant surprises, both for the policyholder drafting a beneficiary clause and for the heir handling an estate.

## Life insurance and inheritance: the principles to know

A life insurance contract rests on a specific legal mechanism: the capital does not belong to the policyholder at the time of death, it is stipulated for the benefit of a third party named in the beneficiary clause. This mechanism, known as a stipulation for the benefit of a third party, explains why life insurance is treated as outside the estate under civil law.

In practice, this means the capital paid to the beneficiary does not pass through the estate and is not subject to sharing among heirs, even forced heirs. A policyholder can therefore freely name someone with no family tie, a friend or an association, without the legal heirs being able to object, except where premiums are clearly excessive relative to the policyholder's wealth and income.

This civil rule does not, however, exempt the transfer from a dedicated tax regime. Sums transferred through life insurance are indeed taxed, but under a system distinct from standard inheritance tax, generally more favorable and adjustable depending on the age at which payments were made.

## How to name the beneficiary of a life insurance contract

The beneficiary clause determines who will receive the capital when the policyholder dies. It can name one or several people specifically, or use a standard wording such as "my spouse, failing that my children born or yet to be born, failing that my heirs." This wording deserves careful attention, since it directly determines how the capital is distributed and how it is taxed.

### Beneficiaries exempt from tax

The **surviving spouse** and the **partner in a civil solidarity pact (PACS)** benefit from a full exemption, regardless of the amount transferred. This rule mirrors the one applicable under standard inheritance tax and makes life insurance particularly well suited to protecting a spouse within a couple.

The beneficiary clause can be changed at any time, as long as the policyholder is alive and legally capable, by sending an amendment to the insurer or filing a clause with a notary. An imprecise or outdated clause (still naming a former spouse after a divorce, for example) is a frequent source of disputes when the contract is settled.

## What happens when the policyholder dies

The policyholder's death triggers the winding-up of the contract. The insurer must be notified, generally by a relative or by the notary handling the estate, upon presentation of a death certificate.

The insurer then locates the named beneficiary or beneficiaries, verifies their identity and gathers the documents required for payment: proof of identity, bank details and, in some cases, an affidavit of inheritance. The capital is paid **outside the estate**, which means the beneficiary does not need to wait for the estate to be settled to receive the funds, an advantage that is often decisive when the estate is complex or contested.

<div class="callout-asavoir">
The statutory deadline for paying out the capital is generally one month from the date the insurer receives the complete set of supporting documents. Beyond that, late-payment interest automatically accrues in the beneficiary's favor.
</div>

## Life insurance taxation on death: payments before and after age 70

The tax regime that applies on death depends on one central criterion: the policyholder's age at the time of each payment, not the date of death. Two regimes coexist, both defined by the French General Tax Code.

### Tax allowances depending on the age of payments

| Age when the payment was made | Allowance per beneficiary | Tax beyond the allowance |
|---|---|---|
| Before age 70 (Article 990 I of the Tax Code) | 152,500 euros per beneficiary | 20 percent up to 700,000 euros, then 31.25 percent beyond |
| After age 70 (Article 757 B of the Tax Code) | 30,500 euros, shared among all beneficiaries, on payments only | Standard inheritance tax based on family relationship, on the excess portion |

For premiums paid before the policyholder turns 70, each beneficiary has their own 152,500 euro allowance. Beyond that, the excess portion is taxed at a flat rate, unrelated to standard inheritance tax scales, which often makes the arrangement more advantageous than a conventional transfer.

For payments made after age 70, the regime is considerably less favorable: an overall allowance of only 30,500 euros applies, to be split among all beneficiaries of the contract regardless of their number. Crucially, this allowance only covers the amount of premiums paid: interest and capital gains generated by the contract after that date remain entirely tax exempt, which limits the impact of the rule for older contracts that have generated significant returns.

<div class="callout-attention">
These two allowances do not combine for the same beneficiary, but they apply contract by contract and insurer by insurer where payments are split before and after age 70. A case-by-case review of the payment timeline is worthwhile before any wealth-planning decision.
</div>

## Special cases: surviving spouse and no named beneficiary

Beyond the full exemption granted to the surviving spouse and PACS partner, certain situations deserve specific attention.

When **no beneficiary is validly named**, for example if the clause was left blank or if every named beneficiary died before the policyholder with no substitution clause, the capital of the contract **is folded back into the estate**. It then loses its specific tax advantage and is transferred under standard inheritance rules, with ordinary inheritance tax applying based on the family relationship with the deceased.

Conversely, when a beneficiary expressly waives the benefit of the contract, their share is passed on to the other named beneficiaries or, failing that, folded back into the estate under the terms of the clause.

## How to claim a death benefit on a life insurance policy

A relative who suspects they may be the beneficiary of a life insurance policy without certainty can contact AGIRA (the French association for managing insurance risk information), an organization tasked with tracing unclaimed contracts. The request is made by letter along with a death certificate, and insurers contacted have a statutory deadline to respond.

Once the contract has been identified, a few steps help speed up payment:
- Gather the death certificate and a valid proof of identity before contacting the insurer.
- Check with the notary handling the estate whether a beneficiary clause was filed separately from the contract.
- Provide bank details promptly to avoid any administrative delay in the transfer.
- Keep a copy of all correspondence with the insurer in case of a dispute over identifying the beneficiary.
