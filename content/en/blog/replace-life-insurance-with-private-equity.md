> **In short:**
> 1. Replacing a French life insurance policy with **private equity** only makes sense partially: life insurance keeps four advantages unlisted assets do not offer, namely the euro fund guarantee (2.6% paid in 2025), availability, social charges kept at 17.2% in 2026 and an estate allowance of 152,500 euros per beneficiary.
> 2. Three routes exist: stay in the euro fund, hold unlisted unit-linked funds inside the policy (**Ramify**, **Altaroc**'s evergreen fund), or make a **partial withdrawal** to subscribe directly on a platform (**Fundora**, **Moonfare**, **Altaroc** directly).
> 3. **Fundora** is the most accessible direct route in the panel: subscriptions pooled in an **FPCI** combined with an **SPV**, sharply lowered entry threshold, management under mandate by **Kyoseil Asset Management**, AMF-authorised (GP-99040). Moonfare starts at 50,000 euros, Altaroc at 100,000 euros directly.
> 4. On a policy older than eight years, a 20,000 euro partial withdrawal containing 4,615 euros of gains costs about 794 euros in social charges for a single person, with the 4,600 euro allowance covering income tax. French private equity delivered a 10.7% net annual IRR over ten years to the end of 2025 according to France Invest and EY.

## Comparison table: three ways to move from life insurance to private equity

| Criterion | Keep the euro fund | Unlisted unit-linked funds in the policy | Partial withdrawal and direct subscription |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platforms involved | Any insurer | Ramify, Altaroc evergreen fund, funds listed by the insurer | Fundora, Moonfare, Altaroc Odyssey |
| Entry threshold | None | From 1,000 euros at Ramify | Sharply lowered at Fundora through pooling, 50,000 euros at Moonfare, 100,000 euros at Altaroc |
| Share exposed to unlisted assets | 0% | A fraction of the policy, often 5% to 20% | 100% of the reallocated amount |
| Fee layers | Policy | Policy, fund and underlying fund | Access structure and target fund |
| Liquidity | Withdrawal at any time | Subject to fund liquidity | Capital locked for 5 to 10 years |
| Tax on gains | Life insurance regime, 17.2% social charges | Life insurance regime, 17.2% social charges | 31.4% flat tax in 2026, or income tax exemption for some tax FPCIs after five years |
| Estate transfer | 152,500 euro allowance per beneficiary | Same | Standard inheritance rules |
| Reference return | 2.6% in 2025 | Depends on funds | 10.7% net annual IRR over ten years for French PE |

The table sums up the trade-off between **private equity and life insurance** for a saver looking to boost a policy. The right-hand column is the only one that amounts to actually replacing part of the policy, since the whole reallocated amount works in unlisted assets. The first two columns keep the wrapper and its advantages, at the cost of limited or zero exposure.

## What life insurance provides that private equity does not replace

The urge to leave life insurance almost always comes from euro fund returns. They paid **2.6%** on average in 2025, net of management fees and before social charges, for the third year running according to France Assureurs, and 2.63% according to the ACPR. Net inflows into French life insurance nonetheless reached 50.6 billion euros in 2025, a sign that the wrapper remains the bedrock of French savings.

### Four advantages worth keeping

| Life insurance asset | Key 2026 figure | Private equity equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Capital guarantee on the euro fund | 2.6% average return in 2025 | No guarantee, capital loss possible |
| Availability | Partial withdrawal at any time | Capital locked for 5 to 10 years |
| Social charges | 17.2%, excluded from the 2026 increase | 18.6% on capital gains |
| Estate transfer | 152,500 euros per beneficiary for contributions before age 70 | No specific allowance |

France's 2026 Social Security financing law raised the CSG on capital income, bringing social charges to 18.6%, but explicitly excluded life insurance from the increase. The tax gap between the wrapper and an investment held directly has therefore widened this year.

A detailed comparison of wrappers is available in the analysis [PEA vs life insurance](/en/blog/pea-vs-life-insurance/), and a ranking of policies in the guide to the [best life insurance in 2026](/en/blog/best-life-insurance-2026/).

### What private equity brings in return

French private equity shows a 10.7% net IRR per year over ten years to the end of 2025, against 9.5% for the CAC 40 over the same period. Correlation with listed markets remains low, and the asset class gives access to growing companies absent from the stock market.

> "The IRR stands at 10.8% net per year since inception and 10.7% over a ten-year horizon, against 9.5% for the CAC 40 over the same period."
> France Invest and EY, study on the net performance of French private equity at the end of 2025, June 2026

That average covers very wide dispersion. Top-quartile funds exceed a 20% net IRR while the bottom quartile ends in negative territory. The quality of fund selection therefore matters more than the decision to invest in the asset class.

## The real cost of a partial exit from life insurance

There is no transfer from a French life insurance policy into a private equity fund. The exit goes through a withdrawal, of which only the gains portion is taxed. The calculation follows a simple ratio: gains in the withdrawal equal the amount withdrawn multiplied by total gains, divided by the policy value.

### Worked example on a policy older than eight years

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cumulative contributions | 100,000 euros |
| Policy value | 130,000 euros |
| Total gains | 30,000 euros |
| Partial withdrawal requested | 20,000 euros |
| Gains portion of the withdrawal | 4,615 euros |
| Annual allowance, single person | 4,600 euros |
| Income tax at 7.5% on the excess | about 1 euro |
| Social charges at 17.2% | about 794 euros |

For a couple taxed jointly, the allowance rises to 9,200 euros and fully covers income tax on this withdrawal. Social charges are not due a second time on the euro fund portion already charged each year, which often reduces the actual bill.

### Spreading the withdrawal over several years

The allowance renews every calendar year. A saver who wants to reallocate 40,000 euros therefore benefits from splitting the withdrawal over two tax years, which doubles the allowance used. This split fits private equity well, where spreading subscriptions over several vintages reduces dependence on a single deployment year.

A full withdrawal should be avoided in most cases. It loses the policy's opening date, and with it the eight-year tax seniority and, for contributions made before age 70, the estate framework.

## Fundora, the platform for the private equity pocket outside life insurance

For the partial withdrawal followed by a direct subscription, **Fundora** is the platform requiring the least capital for fully unlisted exposure. A French platform reporting more than 40,000 users and more than 80 million euros raised, it selects private equity funds from the global top quartile and presents its strategies on the [Fundora website](https://www.fundora.fr/).

The mechanism relies on pooling. Subscriptions from several investors are grouped in an **FPCI** combined with an **SPV**, which invests directly in the target funds. Institutional funds usually require 200,000 euros to 1 million euros per commitment. With this structure, the entry threshold is sharply lowered, the minimum amount varying with the strategy open for subscription.

Fundora identifies and offers the strategies, while actual management is carried out by **Kyoseil Asset Management** under a mandate. Kyoseil AM is a portfolio management company authorised by the AMF under number GP-99040.

### What this route changes compared with a unit-linked fund

- **100% of the reallocated amount** is invested in unlisted assets, against a fraction of the policy with a unit-linked fund.
- **No insurance wrapper fees** on top of fund fees.
- **Diversification possible with limited capital**: more than 35 strategies offered since launch, across venture, growth, LBO, secondaries and private debt, with target multiples of 2.5x to 4x depending on the strategy.
- **Trade-off**: capital locked for five to ten years, standard tax on capital gains and no capital guarantee.

The head-to-head between the two platform models is detailed in the comparison [Fundora vs Ramify for private equity](/en/blog/fundora-vs-ramify-private-equity/).

## Other platforms depending on the chosen route

### To stay within life insurance: Ramify and Altaroc's evergreen fund

Ramify lists unlisted funds from **1,000 euros** and offers an Elite managed portfolio from 10,000 euros, through life insurance, PER or a securities account. Altaroc has designed an evergreen fund intended for life insurance and PER. These solutions keep the estate allowance and the 17.2% social charges, but unlisted exposure remains partial.

### For larger portfolios: Moonfare and Altaroc Odyssey

Moonfare opens funds run by major international managers through feeder funds, from **50,000 euros**. Altaroc launches an annual Odyssey multi-fund vintage, from **100,000 euros** directly. These tickets assume financial wealth of several hundred thousand euros to keep the unlisted pocket reasonable.

The full landscape of players is covered in the comparison of [private equity platforms for retail investors in France](/en/blog/private-equity-platforms-france-2026/).

## What share of a life insurance policy to reallocate by profile

| Profile | Indicative unlisted pocket | Best-suited route |
|---|---|---|
| Aged 30 to 45, long horizon, emergency savings in place | 10% to 20% of financial wealth | Partial withdrawal and direct subscription, several vintages |
| Aged 45 to 60, preparing retirement | 5% to 15% | Mix of direct subscription and unlisted unit-linked funds |
| Over 65, estate transfer goal | 0% to 10% | Unlisted unit-linked funds within the policy, to keep the estate framework |
| Financial wealth above 500,000 euros | 10% to 20% | Direct subscription, complemented by Moonfare or Altaroc |

These ranges are indicative and calculated on total financial wealth, not on the life insurance policy alone. The reasoning matches the one detailed for [investing in private equity for retirement](/en/blog/private-equity-retirement/), where the exit horizon determines the bearable share.

### Mistakes to avoid

- Closing a policy older than eight years to fund an unlisted investment.
- Reallocating emergency savings or money for a project due within ten years.
- Comparing the euro fund return with a target multiple without accounting for the guarantee and liquidity.
- Choosing a platform on entry ticket alone, without checking the share actually invested in unlisted assets or the asset manager's authorisation.
- Concentrating everything on a single strategy or vintage.
