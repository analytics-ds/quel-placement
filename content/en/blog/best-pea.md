Finding the best PEA means comparing several institutions on precise criteria rather than relying on a generic ranking. Fees, the available investment universe, the management style and taxation vary significantly from one provider to another. This guide details the criteria that matter when choosing the Plan d'Épargne en Actions best suited to a given profile.

## The essential criteria for identifying the best PEA

The most suitable PEA is not necessarily the one with the lowest fees: it depends on the saver's profile, their autonomy in managing the account, and their investment objectives. Comparing a [PEA to a life insurance policy](/en/blog/pea-vs-life-insurance/) already helps confirm that the chosen wrapper matches the intended objective, before even comparing institutions with each other.

Several criteria need to be examined together: the fee structure, the breadth of the investment universe, the management style on offer (self-directed or managed), the quality of the tracking interface, and finally the transfer terms if the saver already holds a PEA elsewhere. None of these criteria should be ignored in favour of a single marketing argument.

The investor's profile strongly shapes the answer. An autonomous saver, comfortable tracking their own positions, will mainly look for low brokerage fees and a wide choice of eligible stocks and ETFs. A less available profile will favour managed options or a PEA assurance, even if that means accepting somewhat higher management fees in exchange for support.

## Fees to compare between different PEAs

Fees are the first point of comparison, since they directly weigh on net performance over the long term. Several categories should be distinguished before choosing an institution.

**Brokerage fees** apply to each purchase or sale of securities and vary according to the order type and the listing market. **Account-keeping fees**, sometimes called custody fees, are charged annually by some institutions, while others have removed them. **Inactivity fees** may apply after a prolonged period without transactions, a clause worth checking before opening an account. Finally, **management fees** only apply to managed formulas or PEA assurance contracts invested in unit-linked funds.

Some institutions advertise offers presented as **free**, which in practice covers different realities: no account-keeping fees but brokerage fees maintained, or the reverse. The comparison should therefore focus on the total cost for a given usage profile, not on a single isolated pricing line. The cheapest offer at opening is not systematically the most advantageous choice to invest in the stock market over the long term, particularly if it charges more for recurring transactions.

## Bank PEA, insurance PEA and managed accounts: which formula to choose

The standard bank PEA allows direct holding of stocks, eligible ETFs and certain funds, with self-directed management by the account holder. This is the most common and flexible formula for an investor who wants to keep control over their own decisions.

Opening a PEA follows a uniqueness rule: each taxpayer may only hold a single standard PEA, opened with a single institution at a time. This rule applies per person rather than per tax household, which allows two spouses to each hold their own PEA in parallel, potentially supplemented by a PEA-PME.

The **managed PEA** delegates investment decisions to a professional according to a risk profile defined when the account is opened. This formula suits savers who lack the time or knowledge to follow the markets, in exchange for annual management fees added on top of the underlying brokerage fees.

The **PEA assurance**, offered as a capitalisation contract, works differently from a bank PEA: securities are held through the insurer rather than directly. It is an alternative worth considering alongside the [best life insurance policy](/en/blog/best-life-insurance-2026/), since both wrappers can meet complementary diversification needs depending on the desired share of equities in the overall portfolio.

## Deposit limits and investment universe of the PEA

The PEA is subject to deposit limits that vary according to its version. Since the 2019 PACTE law, these limits are as follows:

| PEA type | Deposit limit | Eligible public |
|---|---|---|
| Standard PEA | 150,000 euros | Any adult tax resident in France |
| PEA-PME | 225,000 euros (combined with the standard PEA) | Complement to the standard PEA, focused on SMEs |
| Young person's PEA | 20,000 euros | 18-25 year olds attached to their parents' tax household |

The PEA's investment universe remains limited to European stocks and eligible ETFs tracking European equity indices, which effectively excludes direct holdings of American or Asian stocks. Some ETFs nonetheless allow indirect exposure to indices outside the eurozone while remaining eligible for the PEA, through a replication method that still meets the regulatory threshold of European equity investment required for this wrapper. Savers looking for broader diversification outside the PEA can complement it with the [best savings account](/en/blog/best-savings-account-2026/) for the most liquid portion of their savings, since the PEA is designed for a long investment horizon.

## Online bank or traditional bank to open a PEA

The choice of institution goes beyond fees alone. Online banks generally offer more modern tracking interfaces and more competitive brokerage fees, which suits autonomous profiles used to managing their investments without an intermediary.

Traditional banks retain an advantage for savers who want a dedicated advisor and ongoing support, particularly for major rebalancing decisions. This support does come at a cost, which is generally reflected in a higher fee structure.

An existing PEA can be transferred to another institution without losing its tax seniority, provided it is a formal transfer rather than a closure followed by a new opening. The original opening date is retained in the new contract, which preserves the 5-year count required for exemption from income tax on capital gains.
